Stroke Admission Orders

1 Admission and Workup

  • For routine stroke admissions, use order set “NEURO Stroke Ischemic/TIA Admission” Post-TNK or mechanical thrombectomy patients use order set “NEURO Stroke POST Thrombolytic Administration”
  • Bedside dysphagia screen (YALE) performed by ED//ICU/floor nursing, must be documented in chart (see Flowsheets). If the patient fails, he/she MUST have formal swallow evaluation.
  • Patients who fail dysphagia screen must be strict NPO (this includes oral medications!) until they are cleared by speech/swallow
  • Make sure any medication orders accurately reflect the patient’s po status (eg oral or DHT route)

1.1 Medications

  • ASA 325 mg PO if swallowing, 300 mg PR if not (unless on anticoagulation or TNK <24 hours)

  • High-intensity statin (atorvastatin or rosuvastatin)

  • Can consider NS @ 100 cc/hr (careful with patients with CHF or on HD). Avoid hypotonic fluids.

  • acetaminophen 650 mg q4h prn for temp >100.4

1.2 Floor orders

  • Sliding scale insulin and fingersticks (q6h if NPO)

  • Neurochecks q4h on floor (q2h in IMC, q1h in NICU)

  • UA/UDS

  • CXR

  • EKG in ED, telemetry/monitoring after admission

  • SCD (all) plus Heparin SQ 5,000 units q8h (unless TNK in last 24 hours)

  • Permissive hypertension (SBP<220, DBP<110), hold home BP meds (except ½ dose β-blocker)

    • Exception: BP goal will be lower following TNK (<180/105) or mechanical thrombectomy (goal depends on procedure result, discuss with fellow/attending)

  • Labs: lipid panel, HgbA1c, RPR, liver function tests, troponin, TSH, folate/B12

  • If concerned for endocarditis, send serial blood cultures

  • Transthoracic echocardiogram with ultrasound contrast

  • Dual energy CT Head within 24 hours (can be as soon as 18, can discuss with fellow/attending)

  • MRI brain (without contrast unless hemorrhage or concern for inflammatory/neoplastic cause)

  • MRA head and neck vs. carotid ultrasound/transcranial dopplers if patient has not had CTA head/neck

Certain patients (e.g., younger patients) may require TEE, hypercoagulable or vasculitis workup. Discuss with attending before ordering.

Important

Hypercoagulable panel:
Arterial: anticardiolipin, DRVVT, beta-2 microglobulin
Venous: prothrombin gene mutation, Factor V Leiden mutation, Protein C activity, Protein S activity, antithrombin III activity, HIT antibody (in appropriate clinical setting).

2 Stroke mechanisms

Most common mechanisms of stroke include:

  • Cardioembolism (~20% ischemic stroke): Atrial fibrillation (most common, esp in older adults), CHF/CM with low EF, LV or LAA thrombus, cardiac tumor (myxoma/fibroelastoma), mechanical valve, endocarditis (infectious or marantic [autoimmune/malignancy]), paradoxical embolism (PFO, pulm AVM)
  • Large-artery atherosclerosis (~15%) – extracranial or intracranial (affects treatment choice)
  • Small-vessel disease (~25%) – must be subcortical/brainstem/cerebellum. Small cortical strokes are generally embolic. Lacunar strokes are usually linked to uncontrolled vascular risk factors.
  • Rare causes (<5%) – undiagnosed malignancy, vasculitis, mitochondrial metabolism disorders, genetic causes
  • Cryptogenic stroke (~20-30%) – no cause found despite thorough workup. If embolic-appearing, may be ESUS (embolic stroke of undetermined source), consider long-term Afib monitoring, no role for empiric AC per ESUS trials to date.

3 Stroke in Young

In addition to conventional stroke mechanisms, consider the following for patients <60 years old:

Cardiac disease (including congenital, rheumatic valve disease, mitral valve prolapse, patent foramen ovale, endocarditis, atrial myxoma, arrhythmias, cardiac surgery)

Large vessel disease

  • Premature atherosclerosis – consider radiation vasculopathy in patients with previous head/neck irradiation. Also consider HIV
  • Dissection (spontaneous or traumatic)
  • Inherited metabolic diseases (homocystinuria, Fabry’s, pseudoxanthoma elasticum, MELAS syndrome)
  • Fibromuscular dysplasia
  • Infection (bacterial, fungal, tuberculosis, syphilis, Lyme)
  • Vasculitis (collagen vascular diseases — systemic lupus erythematosus, rheumatoid arthritis, Sjögren’s syndrome, polyarteritis nodosa; Takayasu’s disease, Wegener’s syndrome, cryoglobulinemia, sarcoidosis, inflammatory bowel disease, isolated central nervous system angiitis)
  • Moyamoya disease
  • Toxic (illicit drugs — cocaine, heroin, phencyclidine; therapeutic drugs — L-asparaginase, cytosine arabinoside)

Small vessel disease

  • Vasculopathy (infectious, noninfectious, microangiopathy)

Hematologic disease

  • Sickle-cell disease, leukemia, thrombocytosis, polycythemia vera
  • Hypercoagulable states (antiphospholipid antibody syndromes, deficiency of antithrombin III or protein S or C, resistance to activated protein C, increased factor VIII, MTHFR)
  • DIC, TTP
  • Venous occlusion (dehydration, parameningeal infection, meningitis, neoplasm, polycythemia, leukemia, inflammatory bowel disease)
