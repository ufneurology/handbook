Lumbar Puncture

1 Contraindications

  • Skin infection over puncture site
  • Plt < 50K
  • INR > 1.5
  • Increased ICP (get prior head CT in age >60, immunocompromised, h/o CNS disease, abnormal neuro exam including poor mental status)

2 Equipment Checklist

(before you put on the sterile gloves)

  • Consent
  • Kit
  • 22 gauge 3.5” needle
  • Gauze
  • Extra sample containers
  • Gloves, mask
  • Chlorhexidine swabs
  • IV meds if needed
  • Assistance holding the patient if needed
Important

Remember: signed consent in chart, bedside nurse at bedside, procedure time-out performed

3 Opening Pressure

Measured in the lateral decubitus position with legs straightened (bent legs can falsely elevate it).

Population Normal OP
Infants 110 mm H₂O
Children 150 mm H₂O
Adults 180 mm H₂O
Obese adults 250 mm H₂O
  • Low values: usually due to prior LP; can be seen in spontaneous dural tears
  • High values: hydrocephalus, idiopathic intracranial hypertension (pseudotumor), cryptococcal meningitis

4 Protein

  • Increased by 1 mg/dL for every 1000 RBCs
  • Isolated increase is a nonspecific abnormality seen in any process that disrupts the blood-brain barrier (e.g., diabetes complicated by cerebrovascular disease, aging itself)
  • Increased without a corresponding elevation in cell count = Albumino-cytologic dissociation (classic picture of Guillain-Barré)
  • Super high protein (in the 2–3 gram range) occurs in states of very low CSF flow with partial obstruction, often due to tumor

5 Glucose

Should be about 2/3 of that in blood. Low values (Hypoglycorrhachia) are never good.

Possibilities include: tumor, bacterial meningitis, some fungal meningitis, and sarcoidosis.

6 Cell Count

  • Send the first and last tubes. RBCs will decrease between the two in traumatic taps, but remain high if there are truly RBCs in the CSF.
  • Determine the true number of CSF WBCs by subtracting 1 WBC for every 700 RBCs (assuming normal serum WBCs).
  • Cells disintegrate in CSF that sits around for more than 4 hours before laboratory analysis, resulting in “pseudonormalization” of CSF.

7 Micro / Infectious Testing

Each test takes about 1 cc of CSF. Many tests require approval. A good technique is to send a large sample to the lab and add on tests as necessary/approved.

  • Beware of possible false positive VDRL and EBV PCR and false negative HSV PCR if tap is traumatic
  • PCR testing should be done for suspected HSV1 and HSV2 DNA (HSV2 can reactivate with fever and may be detected by PCR in the CSF of patients with other etiologies for encephalitis). Also useful for VZV, enteroviruses, HIV-1 RNA, EBV DNA (seen in primary CNS lymphoma in HIV patients), West Nile virus DNA, CMV DNA, JC virus, and HHV-6 DNA. PCR is not useful for Lyme.
  • Intrathecal anti-borrelial antibody is currently the best test for CNS Lyme testing.
  • Latex particle agglutination tests available for many bacterial antigens: Neisseria, Streptococcus pneumoniae, H. influenzae type B, group B streptococci and E. coli — useful for rapid diagnosis in patients who have been pretreated and whose Gram stain and cultures are negative. Highly specific and can be done within one hour, but not highly sensitive. A negative test does not exclude infection.
  • No rapidly available PCR exists for bacterial DNA
  • Cryptococcal antigen testing is highly specific, sensitive and fast.

8 Other Studies

  • RT-QuiC assay for Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease can be falsely positive in HSV encephalitis, tuberculous meningitis and degenerative disorders. Interpret in light of clinical symptoms and EEG abnormalities. MUST be sent to outside center — ensure all other CSF labs are either processed in house or ordered as send-outs prior to this. CANNOT be added on, and will require repeat LP.
  • CSF protein electrophoresis (includes oligoclonal bands): done for suspected MS. Requires serum protein electrophoresis for comparison (make sure blood sample is also sent). Bands are not specific for MS.
