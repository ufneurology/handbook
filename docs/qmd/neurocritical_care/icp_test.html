ICP and Herniation Management

1 Why does ICP increase?

  • Intracranial Pressure is dictated by the Kellie-Monroe Doctrine:
    The cranium is non-expandable, so the pressure inside the cranium is directly related to the amount of it’s contents Parenchyma<->Blood<->CSF
  • If the equilibrium is disrupted by hemorrhage, edema or a tumor, pressure inside the cranium will increase
  • If left untreated, parenchyma will eventually herniate

2 When to suspect elevated ICP?

Clinical exam can be non-specific, you have to be aware of patients at risk of having increased ICP such as: Severe TBI, CVST, large vessel occlusions, intraparenchymal hemorrhages, liver failure patients awaiting transplant

  • Sensitive but non-specific signs: Agitation, low GCS, N/V, hiccups, yawning
  • Specific but not sensitive signs: Cushing Triad (elevated BP, bradycardia, respiratory depression)
  • Helpful complementary bedside measurements: NPI < 4, Optic Nerve Sheath Diameter > 0.55

3 Herniation Syndromes

Syndrome Exam Findings Cause of Exam Finding
Subfalcine Contralateral/bilateral leg weakness, urinary retention/incontinence Cingulate gyrus displaced under the anterior falx cerebri (compressing the anterior cerebral artery)
Transtentorial Abnormal flexor to extensor posturing → impaired upgaze → respiratory/cardiac arrest Progressive downward displacement and compression of the midbrain, then pons, through the tentorial notch
Uncal Ipsilateral pupillary dilation → CN III palsy → contralateral hemiparesis → ipsilateral hemiparesis (false-localizing sign) Compression of CN III against the tentorial edge → compression of the ipsilateral cerebral peduncle/corticospinal tract → compression of the contralateral cerebral peduncle (Kernohan’s notch)
Cerebellar Tonsillar Downbeat nystagmus → Cushing’s triad (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respiration) → respiratory arrest Cerebellar tonsils herniate through the foramen magnum → compression of PICA, vertebral arteries, and anterior spinal artery → infarction of the medulla, cerebellum, and upper cervical cord

3.1 If any concern for herniation, sustained elevation of ICP:

  • Call to Neurocritical care fellow
  • Ensure appropriate vascular access, consider need for additional PIV and/or stat central line
  • Consider neuroprotective intubation
  • Order CBC, CMP, Ammonia, Serum Osmoles
  • STAT CT Head without contrast

3.2 CT Imaging Findings

  • Sulcal Effacement
  • Basal Cistern Effacement
  • Midline Shift

4 Tiered Approach to ICP Management

4.1 How to use the Tier approach

  • Tiered approach derives from expert consensus, use judiciously.

  • The higher the tier, the greater potential for harm

  • When advancing a tier, continue all interventions of the previous tier.

  • It’s OK to mix approaches from different tiers depending on clinical situation, remember this is only a consensus between specialists

What’s Known to Cause Harm

  • Mannitol as continuous drip

  • Scheduled hypertonic saline

  • Furosemide

  • Steroids (except bacterial meningitis and CNS neoplasm)

  • Temperature < 35 °C

  • CPP > 90 mmHg

# Increased ICP Management

## How to use the Tier approach
-   The higher the tier, the greater potential for harm
-   When advancing a tier, **continue all interventions of the previous tier**

## Before Escalating Care
- Re-examine the patient
- Consider CT head
- Check extracranial causes of elevated ICP (sodium, ammonia, BUN, glucose)

Tier 0
- Assess ABCs
- HOB 30–45°
- Head midline
- SpO₂ > 94%
- ETCO₂ monitoring
- Temp < 37.8 °C
- Arterial line
- Q1h neurochecks + pupillometry; contact Neurology if NPi < 4
- q6h labs with Na and serum osmoles
- Minimize noxious stimuli
- Avoid hypotonic fluids
- Avoid/correct hyponatremia

Tier 1
- Optimize sedation to **RASS –5**
- ↑ Analgesia, start fentanyl gtt
- PaCO₂ 35–38 mmHg / ETCO₂ 32–35 mmHg
- EEG monitoring indicated
- Hypertonic bolus:
  - Mannitol 0.25–1 g/kg IV — **avoid if serum osmoles > 320**
  - 23.4% NaCl 30 mL IV push over 2–5 min (central line only) — **avoid if Na > 160**
  - 3% NaCl 250 mL over 30 min — **avoid if Na > 160**
- Consult Neurosurgery for EVD

Tier 2
- Temporizing hypocapnia: PaCO₂ 32–35 mmHg / ETCO₂ 28–32 mmHg
- Consider trial of paralytics in adequately sedated patients
- If ICP controlled, consider short-term continuous infusion

Tier 3
- Pentobarbital coma
- Therapeutic hypothermia 35–36 °C
