Acute Headache Management

1 HISTORY

  • New headache or history of headaches
  • Location, quality, severity
  • Onset (sudden or gradual)
  • Aura
  • Transient visual obscurations
  • Associated features (nausea/vomiting/photophobia/phonophobia/kinesophobia/diplopia/tinnitus) AND autonomic features (tearing, sinus pressure/fullness, eye redness, sweating)
  • What medications and how much have they taken for the headache
  • Worsening factors (e.g., lying down, standing up, straining)
  • Diurnal pattern (e.g., worse in the morning)
  • OCP or Vitamin A/retinoid use

1.1 Chronic headache syndrome

Ask about family history of headaches and screen for lifestyle factors: caffeine use, sleep, hydration status, frequency of analgesic use, relation to menses, stress, relation to weather.

1.1.1 New headache syndrome

Screen for red flags such as recent head or neck trauma, chiropractor visits, fevers or chills, recent travel or sick contacts, altered mental state, neck stiffness, focal neurologic or visual deficits.

1.2 New headache in the elderly

Screen for GCA symptoms such as fever, weight loss, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, proximal limb myalgias, constitutional symptoms.

2 EXAM

Vital signs with particular attention to blood pressure and temperature, neurologic exam including a fundoscopic and visual fields exam, evaluate for nuchal rigidity, scalp tenderness and Horner’s syndrome.

3 WORKUP

ESR if patient >60 (corrected ESR = (age + 10 if female)/2)

4 PLAN

Dictated by patient’s history and physical.

5 Differential

5.1 Primary (hypersensitization of receptors in meninges)

  1. Migraine: moderate unilateral, F>M, pulsatile, photo/phono, n/v, transient visual obscurations, scintillating scotomas, fortification spectra, + family history, + menstrual, + triggers.
    Subtypes: common (no aura, 50%), classic (aura, 50%), complicated (focal features, <5%), retinal (monocular vision loss), vertiginous, acephalgic (aura without headache)
  2. Tension-type: bilateral, pressure, “hatband”, shoulder/neck tightness
  3. Trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias (Indomethacin responsive aside from cluster)
    • 12a Cluster (15–180 min): severe unilateral, M>F, daily × wks–mths, photo/phono, autonomic features (Horner’s, tearing, rhinorrhea)
    • 12b Paroxysmal Hemicrania (2–30 min): like clusters in women — or — hemicrania continua (constant)
    • 12c SUNCT/SUNA (0–10 min): “short-lasting, unilateral, neuralgiform, conjunctival injection and tearing or autonomic symptoms (1 of those only)”
  4. Occipital Neuralgia: unilateral over occipital region, to eye, lasts seconds
  5. Primary stabbing: migrating pain, “ice-pick feeling” episodic
  6. Hypnic headache: occurs in sleep, improves with caffeine
  7. New daily persistent headache (they can remember when it started to the date)

5.2 Secondary (meningeal stretch, irritation, or ischemia)

  1. Pseudotumor cerebri (IIH): diffuse, photo, tinnitus, vision loss, VI palsy + wt gain/Vit A/meds
  2. Subarachnoid hemorrhage: sudden-onset, WHOL, 10/10, meningismus
  3. Optic neuritis/myositis: painful eye movements +/- vision loss, F>M
  4. Meningitis: diffuse, photo/phono, meningismus, N/V, fever, malaise, irritability
  5. Encephalitis: like meningitis, plus altered mental status, seizures, focal features
  6. Carotid dissection: periorbital/auricular throbbing (carotidynia), Horner’s, TMB, focal features, double lumen on CTA; crescent sign on MRA with fat sats
  7. Temporal arteritis: achy, jaw claudication, myalgias, ESR > age/2 + 10, wt loss
  8. Hypertensive encephalopathy: frontal/occipital, throbbing, +/- confusion
  9. Venous sinus thrombosis: diffuse or focal, seizure, meningismus

6 Targeted Workup

Rule-out Approach
Meningitis/encephalitis HCT → LP (determine aseptic/viral/bacterial/fungal, etc.), broad tx
SAH HCT → LP (HCT less likely to see blood after 48–72 hours)
Pseudotumor HCT → LP (check OP > 20, decubitus position, legs extended after tap better)
Dissection MRI/A neck c/s gad with T1 fat sats; add MRI brain to r/o accompanying infarction
Temporal arteritis TA bruit/tenderness/pulselessness; if ESR high → steroids, bx next day
Hypertensive encephalopathy Watch BP; if focal or AMS → MRI to see RPLS
CVST HCT with contrast (“empty delta sign”) vs MRV
Optic neuritis/myopathy HCT → MRI brain/orbits c/s gad, CK; admit for IV steroids for ON
Acute migraine–tension-type continuum Consider HCT if new HA syndrome

7 Treatment of Acute Inpatient Migraine

7.1 Migraine cocktail (can repeat q8h up to 3 times)

  • IV fluids: 1–2 L NSS or 80–100 cc/hr while hospitalized
  • NSAIDs: 15–30 mg IV ketorolac (caution with GI sx)
  • DA antagonist: 10 mg metoclopramide or prochlorperazine ± 12.5 mg diphenhydramine to prevent EPS
  • Magnesium sulfate: 2 g IV

7.2 If fails, can try

  • Sodium valproate: 500 mg – 1 g IV (caution in patients on topiramate — can cause hyperammonemia); can use this as bridge as well for 3–5 doses q8–12h
  • Methylprednisolone: 200 mg – 1 g IV (caution regarding avascular necrosis, diabetes, active infection)
  • Zyprexa bridge: 5–7.5 mg PO for 7 days (will take days for effect)
  • Droperidol: 2.75–8.25 mg IM once; MUST have cardiac monitoring afterward (Level C evidence)*
  • DHE: See DHE protocol. MUST discuss with attending beforehand.
Warning

Discuss DHE and any high-risk therapies with the attending before administration.

8 Treatment of Cluster Headache

  • 100% O₂ by face mask for 10 min
  • DHE protocol
  • Can also try lidocaine + steroid nerve block or triptans

If prophylaxis is indicated, especially consider:

  • Verapamil
  • Lithium
  • Valproate
