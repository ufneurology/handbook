Speech and Gait

1 Gait

What to observe: Base of stance, Stability of stance (with provocation), Posture, Initiation of gait, Stepping, Base of gait, Stride Length, Arm swing, Turning

Functional tests: Tandem walking, Heel and toe walking

1.1 Common Gait Patterns

  • Antalgic — limping due to pain
  • Ataxic / staggering — wide-based, unsteady, irregular
  • Sensory ataxia — wide-based, unsteady; high-stepping, + Romberg
  • Spastic — stiff, “foot dragging” when ambulating
  • Steppage — foot drop with hip flexor engagement to lift leg
  • Waddling — weak proximal muscles with compensatory circumductive hip movements
  • Scissoring — legs are stiff, knees cross midline when stepping
  • Hyperkinetic — chorea, other abnormal movements that impact gait in any phase
  • Hypokinetic / Parkinsonian — stooped posture, reduced arm swing, shuffling, en-bloc turns
  • Magnetic — feet appear stuck to floor, shuffling when able to walk

2 Speech Production Examination

Components:

  • Respiration - lung dysfunction (ie COPD exacerbation)
  • Phonation - (vocal folds in larynx)
  • Tone / Resonance - ENT changes (larynx, nasal/oral cavity)
  • Articulation - mouth movements (tongue, lips, palate); phonemes are differentiated here and can be tested at bedside
  • Prosody / Rhythm / Flow - pitch, volume, tempo, expression

2.1 Dysarthria Types

  • Flaccid (LMN) — weak, breathy, hyper-nasal, slow; phonation, tone, or articulation primarily affected
  • Spastic (UMN) — strained, harsh, slow; phonation, tone, or articulation primarily affected. Can be unilateral with stroke (UUMN)
  • Ataxic — scanning, jerky, uncoordinated; prosody primarily affected
  • Hypokinetic — reduced mouth movement, monotone (associated with parkinsonism)
  • Hyperkinetic — involuntary movements affecting speech
  • Mixed (e.g., flaccid + spastic in ALS)
