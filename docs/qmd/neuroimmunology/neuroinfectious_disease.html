Neuroinfectious Diseases

1 Meningitis vs. Encephalitis

Meningitis Encephalitis
Symptoms / Signs HA, photophobia / phonophobia, meningismus, N/V, fever, malaise, irritability Similar to meningitis plus acute confusion, personality changes, seizures
Common Bugs Viral: echo, coxsackie, mumps, HSV-2, adenovirus
Bacterial by age:
• <1 mo: GBS, E. coli, Listeria (“hear bells”)
• 1–23 mo: S. pneumo, N. mening, H. flu, E. coli
• 2–50 y: N. mening, S. pneumo
• ≥50 y: S. pneumo, N. mening, Listeria, GNR		 HSV-1 (#1), arboviruses (SLE, LAC, EEE, WEE), WNV, VZV, Legionella
Work-up HCT → LP (routine, HSV PCR, ± crypto / TB / AFB / viral studies) HCT → if no abscess → LP (routine, HSV PCR, ± viral / toxo) → ± MRI and EEG
Treatment Empiric
Viral: supportive (if high suspicion for HSV → acyclovir 10 mg/kg IV q8h)
Bacterial:
• <1 mo: cefotaxime/ceftriaxone + ampicillin
• 1–23 mo: cefotaxime or ceftriaxone + vancomycin
• 2–50 y: ceftriaxone + vancomycin
• ≥50 y: ceftriaxone + vancomycin + ampicillin
Fungal: Amphotericin B + flucytosine + fluconazole
TB: RIPE ± streptomycin
If trauma: use cefepime or meropenem
Dexamethasone 10 mg IV q6h × 4 days beneficial in pneumococcal meningitis (1st dose 20 min before antibiotics)		 By organism
• HSV / VZV: acyclovir 10 mg/kg IV q8h × ≥14 days
• CMV: ganciclovir or foscarnet
• Legionella: azithromycin, quinolone, or tetracyclines
Prognosis • Aseptic / viral: most improve over 2 weeks
• Bacterial: ~20% mortality (S. pneumo worst)		 • HSV & EEE: 70% die untreated, 25% treated
• Others: 5–20% mortality
Important

Start treatment empirically — do not wait for the LP.

2 Serum Studies

2.1 Viruses

Measure IgM and IgG antibodies (acute and convalescent — look for 4-fold rise in IgG):

  • St. Louis encephalitis
  • West Nile virus
  • Eastern & Western Equine encephalitis
  • Japanese encephalitis
  • Dengue fever
  • EBV (VCA IgM/IgG + EBNA)
  • VZV
  • HSV-1 (IgM)
  • Rabies
  • HIV (RNA can be checked early when viral load is high but ELISA is weakly positive or negative)

2.2 Tick-borne Bacteria

  • RMSF: IgG & IgM by indirect immunofluorescence
  • Lyme: ELISA → confirmatory Western blot
  • Ehrlichia: antibodies by indirect fluorescent antibody

2.3 Parasites

Toxoplasma gondii

  • Absence of IgG or IgM antibodies does not exclude toxoplasma encephalitis in AIDS patients
  • If positive in an HIV patient → treat with pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine + folinic acid
  • Incidence is reduced in patients receiving Pneumocystis prophylaxis with sulfadiazine or dapsone + pyrimethamine
  • If toxoplasma serology is negative → check CSF EBV PCR and consider brain biopsy of any accessible mass lesion (to evaluate for lymphoma)

Taenia solium (cysticercosis)

  • ELISA (note: ~50% of patients with solitary lesions will be seronegative)
