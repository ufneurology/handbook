Optic Neuritis

1 Epidemiology

  • Usually ages 20s–40s
  • Female:Male ratio ≈ 2:1

2 Presentation

  • Monocular vision loss / scotoma
  • Red desaturation
  • Painful eye movements (≈ 90%)
  • Uhthoff’s phenomenon

3 Exam

  • Swollen discs in only ~1/3 of cases
  • Central vision loss
  • Poor color plates / red hatpin
  • Relative afferent pupillary defect (+APD)

4 Workup + Admission + Treatment

  1. Order MRI brain and orbits.

  2. Consider MRI of the spine; if no signs or symptoms of myelopathy, this can be done outpatient.

  3. Consider ophthalmology consultation.

  4. Send ON infectious workup (syphilis, bartonella, Lyme) and order CDS1 (CNS demyelinating panel) in serum (AQP4 and MOG).

    • If more atypical (meningitis + TM), can order MAS1 (myelopathy panel) or ENS2 in serum, which includes AQP4, MOG, GFAP, and CRMP5.

  5. Admit for IV Solumedrol 1 g/d × 3–5 days (can also use high-dose oral equivalent).

  6. If bilateral, visual acuity worse than 20/200, or long-segment involvement → strongly consider MOG/AQP4 and discuss concomitant plasma exchange (PLEX).
