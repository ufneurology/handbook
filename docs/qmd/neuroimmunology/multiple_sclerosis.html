Multiple Sclerosis (Flare)

1 Epidemiology

  • Expect 1–2 flares in untreated RRMS patient per year (75% will have at least 1 flare).
  • If they are on high-efficacy drugs the true relapse should be close to 0.

2 Presentation

2.1 Relapse

Patient-reported or objectively observed events typical of an acute inflammatory demyelinating event in the CNS (current or historical), with duration of at least 24 hours, in the absence of fever or infection.

Consider:

  • Vision loss with painful eye movements (optic neuritis)
  • Trigeminal neuralgia
  • Focal weakness
  • Focal numbness
  • Ataxia
  • Paresthesias
  • Incontinence
  • Falls
  • Fatigue

KEY WORDS: NEW or WORSE; 24 hours or more.

2.2 Pseudorelapse / Pseudoflare

Acute worsening of symptoms that is typically associated with an increase in body temperature, or stress in the body, which can be due to pain, stress, infection, exercise, or heat exposure.

3 History

  • Current symptoms (onset, duration, progression)
  • Past neurologic symptoms (if MS suspected)
  • Recent signs of infection (fever, chills, URI symptoms, dysuria)
  • If patient has known MS, have they ever had these exact symptoms before? This may suggest recrudescence due to systemic issue, such as an infection
  • Pregnancy

If known MS — MS history

  • When, where, and how they were diagnosed
  • Type of MS (RRMS, PPMS, SPMS)
  • Medication history and compliance
    • If on Natalizumab, Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) modulators (fingolimod / ozanimod / siponimod) or fumarates (dimethyl fumarate, diroximel fumarate): LAST JCV TEST result
    • These are the 3 DMTs with highest risk of PML. PML can happen on fumarates with grade 3 lymphopenia (ALC of <500)
  • Last exacerbation, last round of steroids
  • If on Tysabri or (S1P) receptor modulators (Fingolimod, ozanimod, siponimod): ask for the last date the drug was administered
    • Watch out for Natalizumab or (S1P) receptor modulators discontinuation rebound (usually around 3 months from last administration)
  • Baseline deficits

Exam

Full physical and neurologic exam with specific attention to:

  • Relative afferent pupillary defect (rAPD)
  • Optic nerve pallor
  • Internuclear ophthalmoplegia (INO)
  • Lhermitte’s sign
  • Spasticity
  • Hyper-reflexia
  • McArdle’s sign (finger extensor weakness on neck flexion)
  • Proprioception

4 Workup ± Admission + Treatment

  1. Rule out infection with UA/UCx & CXR if indicated. If positive, may represent a “pseudoflare” — treat the infection first and see if flare resolves. Patient may also warrant admission to medical service if cannot walk.

  2. Rule out Uhthoff’s phenomenon (unmasking of old flare symptoms due to increased body temperature) — may not warrant acute steroid administration.

  3. If the patient is having NEW or WORSE symptoms, obtain MRIs to rule out new enhancing lesions. If enhancing lesions, then order high-dose steroids.

  4. Consider admission based on following criteria:

    • “Can’t see” (optic neuritis)
    • “Can’t pee” (myelitis, neurogenic bladder)
    • “Can’t eat/swallow”
    • “Can’t walk”
    • “Can’t take care of family”

    If not admitted, can arrange outpatient infusion center IV steroids or the equivalent oral steroids.

    • The equivalent oral steroids is 1250 mg of prednisone for 3–5 days.
    • When you write the prescription write to the pharmacy: “This is not an error: this is the equivalent of 1 gram of methylprednisolone daily for the treatment of MS flare”
    • Consider calling the pharmacy to confirm or write your cellphone in the prescription; more often than not if you do not do this the pharmacist will cancel this thinking it was an error and you will not hear about it until the following week.
    • Send a PPI for steroids at home.

  5. Start IV Solumedrol 1 g/d × 3–5 days if suspect new relapse or enhancing lesions on MRI.

    • No need for oral taper if 5 days or less. (Remember we use a taper for MOGAD or sarcoidosis.)
    • If sent with taper, send PPI and ask them to follow up with PCP about blood glucose and adjustments.
    • Can also give oral equivalent which is 1250 mg of prednisone daily for 3–5 days.

  6. If the patient is on steroids inpatient; prevent steroid side effects with:

    • GI prophylaxis (PPI)
    • Finger stick glucose checks / sliding scale insulin
    • Diabetic diet
    • Mental status checks
    • Consider a sleep aid

  7. Order PT/OT/Rehab service consults immediately if patient is unable to care for self at home on admission.

  8. Continue outpatient symptomatic medications, such as muscle relaxants, SSRIs, stimulants, anticonvulsants, antipsychotics, amantadine, etc. Immunomodulating therapies (Interferons, Glatiramer acetate, Gilenya, Tecfidera, Aubagio) can be continued using home supply.

5 Evidence for Treatment in Acute MS Exacerbations

  • IV steroids may expedite the timing of recovery from a flare
  • IV steroids will not increase the degree of recovery or the course of disease (different from MOGAD or NMO)
  • High-dose oral steroids or IV is equivalent for MS (1250 : 1000 mg)
  • Alternatives to failed IV steroids in patients with tumefactive MS include plasma exchange and Cytoxan (when rapidly decompensating — lesion in the medulla causing respiratory failure for example)
  • In a patient that may have difficulty getting a DMT outpatient (very active and not soon follow-up appointment) can consider 1 g of rituximab once to give time to the outpatient neurologist to get the DMT
  • If going to do rituximab needs the hepatitis B/C antibodies + QuantiFERON testing prior to giving

5.1 If Suspected New Diagnosis

A full differential needs to be considered.

Work-up:

  • MRI brain and C/T spine w/wo contrast + orbits if optic neuritis
  • Cytokine 13 panel (sarcoidosis)
  • Lyme disease
  • RPR/VDRL
  • CDS1 (demyelinating panel that has MOG IgG and AQP4 antibodies in serum)
  • Consider vasculitis and rheum panel (depending on the case)
  • LP (if not classic and does not meet Revised 2025 McDonald Criteria)
    • CSF should be evaluated for basic studies + oligoclonal bands, kappa free light chains (send out lab), and myelin basic protein
