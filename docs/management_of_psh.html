1 Management of PSH

2 Paroxysmal Sympathetic Hyperactivity (PSH)

PSH is a disorder in the regulation of autonomic function with acute brain injury, most notably severe TBI.

  • Recurrent episodes of excessive sympathetic activity that start rapidly, are often induced by stimulation, and resolve spontaneously after a variable period of time or after administration of an abortive medication.
  • Core clinical features include tachycardia, hypertension, tachypnea, hyperthermia, sweating, and/or increased muscle tone with possible dystonic posturing.
  • Associated conditions: brain trauma, nontraumatic forms of acute brain disease including anoxic-ischemic coma after cardiac arrest, intracranial hemorrhage, autoimmune encephalitis, ischemic stroke, hydrocephalus, and cerebral fat embolism syndrome.

2.1 Abortive and Preventive Medications for PSH

Drug Mechanism Dose Clinical effect Side effects
Morphine sulfate Opioid agonist* 2 to 8 mg IV bolus Abortive. Improves most features Respiratory depression, sedation, hypotension, ileus, emesis, histamine release, tolerance
Propofol GABA-A agonist 10 to 20 mg IV bolus or continuous infusion (up to 80 mcg/kg/min) Abortive (bolus) or preventive (infusion). Improves most features Deep sedation (only in intubated/ventilated patients), propofol infusion syndrome
Benzodiazepines GABA-A agonists Diazepam 5–10 mg IV bolus; Lorazepam 1–4 mg IV bolus; Midazolam 2–5 mg IV bolus; Clonazepam 0.5–2 mg q8h enteral Abortive (diazepam, lorazepam, midazolam) or preventive (clonazepam). Less effective than morphine. Diazepam useful for hypertonicity Sedation, respiratory depression, tolerance, bradycardia, hypotension
Dexmedetomidine Central alpha-2 adrenergic receptor agonist 0.2 to 1.5 mcg/kg/hour IV infusion Abortive and preventive. Improves mostly tachycardia and hypertension Bradycardia, hypotension, sedation
Clonidine Central alpha-2 adrenergic receptor agonist 0.1 to 0.3 mg every 6–8 hours enteral (SC option available) Abortive and preventive. Improves mostly tachycardia and hypertension Bradycardia, hypotension, sedation
Propranolol† Noncardioselective beta blocker 20 to 80 mg every 4–6 hours enteral Preventive. Improves tachycardia, hypertension, diaphoresis; may improve dystonia Bradycardia, hypotension, sleep disturbances
Gabapentin Interacts with α2-δ subunit of voltage-gated Ca channels 100 to 300 mg every 8 hours enteral (titrate to 3600–4800 mg/day) Preventive. Improves most features Mild sedation
Bromocriptine Dopamine D2 receptor agonist 1.25 mg every 12 hours enteral (titrate to 20–40 mg/day) Preventive. Effect modest and delayed Confusion, agitation, dyskinesia, nausea/emesis, orthostatic hypotension, may reduce seizure threshold
Baclofen GABA-B agonist 5 mg every 8 hours enteral (titrate to 80 mg/day; intrathecal option available) Preventive. Improves hypertonicity and dystonia Sedation, muscle weakness, risk of withdrawal (especially intrathecal)
Dantrolene Ryanodine receptors in myocytes 0.5 to 2 mg/kg IV every 6–12 hours (max 10 mg/kg/day) Abortive. Improves hypertonicity and dystonia Hepatotoxicity (can be severe), respiratory depression, muscle weakness

Notes: - *Other opioids (e.g., fentanyl) can be used but are usually less effective than morphine. - †Can be replaced with IV labetalol if enteral route unavailable. Cardioselective beta blockers (e.g., metoprolol) are usually much less effective. - Source: Rabinstein AA. Autonomic hyperactivity. Continuum. 2020;26:138.
