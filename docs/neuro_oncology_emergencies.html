Neuro-oncology Emergencies

1 Overview

Brain tumor

  • Direct effects: Epilepsy, Stroke, SIADH, Diabetes insipidus, Behavioral symptoms
  • Indirect effects: VTE, Stroke

Iatrogenic causes

  • Antineoplastic therapies
    • Surgery: Stroke, Cerebral abscess
    • Chemotherapy: Nausea/vomiting, Constipation, HTN, Stroke, PRES, Bowel perforation, Myelotoxicity
    • Radiation: Fatigue, Dermatitis, Memory loss, Hearing loss, Cataracts
  • Supportive therapies
    • Steroids: Cushing syndrome, Infection, Diabetes mellitus, Mood disorder, Myopathy, Tremor
    • AEDs: Sedation, Fatigue, Irritability, Hyponatremia, Rash, Myelotoxicity

1.0.1 Mass Effect

Category Details
Neuro-onc specifics Vasogenic edema: tumor increases vascular permeability → responds to steroids
Acute tx If new lesion and suspicion for CNS lymphoma, consider osmotic therapy as a bridge to urgent biopsy (as dexamethasone can decrease yield of biopsy)
Other Steroids effective dose: dexamethasone typically 4 mg BID–QID; subtherapeutic doses do not treat edema and do not cause return of symptoms if stopped. Schedule at 6 am and 2 pm to avoid insomnia

1.0.2 Herniation

Category Details
Acute tx If chemical or herniation: Dexamethasone 10 mg once, then 4 mg q6h
Other Select patients (e.g. large contrast-enhancing tumors with significant vasogenic edema) may benefit from bevacizumab long-term as can improve edema over days to weeks. If used long-term, discuss with oncology

1.0.3 Hydrocephalus

Category Details
Neuro-onc specifics Communicating hydrocephalus common w/ leptomeningeal carcinomatosis
Acute tx Noncommunicating hydrocephalus: often from structural lesion obstructing ventricular system. May need EVD (page neurosurgery)

1.0.4 Epidural Spinal Cord Compression

Category Details
Acute tx If neurologic deficits or pain associated with compression: immediate treatment with high-dose IV dexamethasone (10 mg once, then 4 mg q6h)
Other Definitive treatment: surgical decompression (page spine as well). May require radiation for treatment or palliation

Neuro-onc specifics Tend to be focal, but can generalize. Risk factors: resection, metabolic derangement, paraneoplastic encephalitis, infection, PRES
Acute tx No benefit from seizure prophylaxis
Other If status epilepticus: treat as needed as remaining seizure activity may outweigh less immediate risk of drug interaction. See AED concise table for further details

Acute tx Involve endocrinology ASAP. Supportive measures, hydrocortisone 100 mg IV (prevent adrenal crisis)
Category Details
Neuro-onc specifics Associated with bevacizumab, sorafenib, cyclophosphamide, L-asparaginase, steroids, gemcitabine, immunosuppressants such as tacrolimus and high-dose steroids, and consequence of uncontrolled hypertension
Acute tx Management of blood pressure
Category Details
Neuro-onc specifics Meningitis: often does not have classic triad and without CSF pleocytosis in cancer patients. Common causative organisms: staph aureus, coag-negative staph, Listeria, and parasitic (toxoplasmosis)
Acute tx Viral encephalitis if one common with immunosuppression: HSV, VZV (consider antibody-IgG and/or testing), CMV, HHV-6, WNV, JC virus (PML). Pneumocystis jirovecii: at risk due to lymphopenia from TMZ or steroids. Candida (thrush): common due to immunosuppression. At risk for cerebral abscess from neurosurgical interventions in context of steroid use
Other Empiric coverage if in hospital or undergoing a neurosurgical procedure, in addition to vancomycin, include cefepime (vs ceftazidime or meropenem) rather than ceftriaxone. If chronic ALC <500 or chronic steroids: consider daily bactrim SS and acyclovir 400 mg daily as prophylaxis

1.0.5 Radiation Necrosis

Category Details
Neuro-onc specifics More likely to occur with doses of radiation >50 Gy
Acute tx Treatment: Dexamethasone 4–24 mg in divided doses to reduce mass effect
Other If cannot tolerate steroids, bevacizumab is an alternative

1.0.6 Thromboembolic Events

Category Details
Neuro-onc specifics High risk for ischemic stroke, pulmonary embolism, DVT; most commonly in postoperative setting and subsequent 6 months
Acute tx Pro-thrombotic therapies: bevacizumab, thalidomide, sorafenib, cisplatin
Other Anticoagulation unless contraindication present

1.0.7 Ischemic Stroke

Category Details
Neuro-onc specifics Chemotherapy: promotes risk of stroke with immune system compromise – bacterial endocarditis, sepsis, VZV vasculopathy. Increased risk in cancer population from: hypercoagulable state, atherosclerosis / vasculopathy – can cause moyamoya, tumor emboli from cardiac metastasis, arterial occlusion by tumor, PRES
Acute tx Steroids relatively contraindicated
Other Otherwise, management per acute ischemic stroke pathway

1.0.8 Intraparenchymal Hemorrhage

Category Details
Neuro-onc specifics Increased risk from chemotherapy, hematologic malignancies (thrombocytopenia, coagulopathy), bevacizumab, radiation vasculopathy, and brain tumors themselves can bleed spontaneously. Most prone to hemorrhage: oligodendroglioma, CNS lymphoma, melanoma, lung, papillary thyroid, renal cell cancer, choriocarcinoma. High-risk hematologic malignancies: APML
Acute tx No different than acute ICH management with blood pressure monitoring, consideration of EVD/VPS if evidence of hydrocephalus, or decompression if evidence of herniation
Other If suspicious IPH suggestive of underlying CNS neoplasm: repeat MRI 6–12 weeks after initial hemorrhage

1.0.9 Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis

Category Details
Neuro-onc specifics Increased risk from hypercoagulable state; treatment of cancer can lead to severe dehydration; certain medications can trigger thrombosis (tamoxifen, L-asparaginase, heparin [especially if HIT]); venous compression by extrinsic metastasis; bacterial infection (mastoiditis or ear infection)
Acute tx Anticoagulation unless contraindication present
Category Details
Neuro-onc specifics See paraneoplastic table for details on specific antibodies and related neurologic syndromes
Acute tx Immunosuppression: high-dose steroids (e.g. 1 g IV methylprednisolone for 5 days). IVIG 2 g/kg divided over 2–5 days. Plasma exchange
Other Treat underlying cancer. If refractory to above therapy: consider rituximab, cyclophosphamide, tocilizumab, cyclosporine
