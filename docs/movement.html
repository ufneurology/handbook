Movement Examination

Author

Luis Silva

Modified

May 26, 2026

1 Coordination

Ataxia — incoordination of movement rhythm through time and space, irrespective of the final target.

Dysmetria — subtype of ataxia: imprecision of targeting movements (over- or under-shooting).

1.1 Midline (Vermal) Signs

  • Trunk ataxia
  • Optic ataxia
  • Ataxic dysarthria (scanning speech)
  • Nystagmus

1.2 Hemispheric (Appendicular) Signs

  • Finger-to-nose (FTN)
  • Heel-to-shin (HTS)
  • Rapid alternating movements (RAM) — dysdiadochokinesia

2 Movement Examination

2.1 Bradykinesia/Hypokinesia

Decrements in speed/amplitude of movement with repetition.
Test with:

  • Finger Tapping
  • Fist ope/close
  • Toe Tapping

2.2 Hyperkinetic Movements

  • Tremor: slow, oscillatory movements with regular interval

    • Resting tremor: arms relaxed in lap or over chair arms
    • Action tremor (postural/kinetic)
      • Egocentric: reaching toward face
      • Allocentric: reaching outward
    • Dystonic tremor: find null point by rotating posture

  • Dystonia — sustained muscle engagement, irregular

  • Myoclonus — brief, irregular, jerky movements in one plane of space; not suppressible

    • Positive myoclonus: in plane of muscle contraction
    • Negative myoclonus: loss of tone (e.g., asterixis)

  • Chorea — multiplanar, semi-brief, irregular, rhythmic movements (complex myoclonus can mimic, look for multiplanar movements)

  • Hemiballismus — large amplitude, irregular

  • Tics — irregular, jerky movements (simple or multiplanar, vocalizations) with URGE followed by relief; suppressible

  • Stereotypies — irregular, complex, fixed/repetitive movements without clear urge; often triggered by negative emotional states
