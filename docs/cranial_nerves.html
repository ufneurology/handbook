Cranial Nerves

1 Cranial Nerve Examination

Key for reduced level of consciousness: * = testable in comatose patients

1.1 CN II (Optic)

  • Fundoscopy: Optic disc, vasculature, venous pulsations, retina
  • Visual acuity: Test with pinhole to correct refractive error
  • Color vision: Ishihara plates (especially red desaturation)
  • Visual fields: Confrontation testing (cuts or neglect); *blink to threat

1.2 CN II/III (Optic & Oculomotor – Pupils)

  • Pupil size, shape, and reactivity to light
  • Relative afferent pupillary defect (swinging flashlight test)
  • Ptosis

1.3 CN III/IV/VI (Oculomotor, Trochlear, Abducens)

  • Extraocular movements:
    • Saccades and smooth pursuit
    • Ductions (each eye separately)
    • Versions (both eyes together)
    • Alignment (cover-uncover / alternate cover test)
  • Note nystagmus, diplopia, or gaze palsy

1.4 CN V (Trigeminal)

  • Sensory: Light touch, pinprick, temperature in V1–V3 distributions
  • *Corneal reflex (afferent limb)
  • Motor: Jaw opening, clenching, deviation on opening

1.5 CN VII (Facial)

  • Motor: Upper and lower face strength (central vs. peripheral pattern)
    • Raise eyebrows, close eyes tightly, smile, puff cheeks, wrinkle forehead
  • Hyperacusis, dysgeusia (anterior 2/3 tongue)
  • *Corneal reflex (efferent limb) / eye closure to prevent corneal dehydration

1.6 CN VIII (Vestibulocochlear)

  • Hearing:
    • Finger rub (high frequency screen)
    • Weber and Rinne tests
    • Response to verbal stimulus
  • Vestibular:
    • Past-pointing
    • Dix-Hallpike maneuver
    • Fukuda marching test
    • *Oculocephalic (doll’s eyes) – assess VOR
    • Nystagmus

1.7 CN IX/X (Glossopharyngeal & Vagus)

  • Palate elevation (“Aaaah”) and uvula deviation
  • *Gag reflex
  • *Cough

1.8 CN XI (Spinal Accessory)

  • Sternocleidomastoid (turn head against resistance – ipsilateral)
  • Trapezius (shrug shoulders against resistance – contralateral)

1.9 CN XII (Hypoglossal)

  • Tongue position at rest, bulk, fasciculations
  • Strength: Push tongue against cheek bilaterally
  • Protrusion (deviation toward weak side)

Document abnormal findings clearly and always correlate with the rest of the neurologic exam.
