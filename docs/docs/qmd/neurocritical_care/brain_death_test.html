Brain Death / BD/DNC – Resident Guide

1 Core Principle

  • Approach every patient with the presumption that the patient is alive and has brain function.
  • The worst error is a false-positive (declaring BD/DNC when the patient does not meet criteria).
  • BD/DNC = permanent loss of all brain function including the brainstem → coma + brainstem areflexia + apnea. The brainstem houses the reticular activating system; if it is not functioning, the patient is in permanent coma.
Before you start

Etiology of the acquired brain injury must be known, catastrophic, and permanent with no possibility of recovery. All confounders must be excluded. It is reasonable to wait 24 hours after completion of all neuroprotective measures (TTM, BP stabilization, clearance of toxins, correction of metabolic derangements) before proceeding.

2 Prerequisites – Must Be Stable

  • Known mechanism that can lead to BD/DNC (TBI, ICH, SAH, HIBI, etc.). Exclude mimics.
  • Physiologic parameters must be normal — this is basic medicine and one of the biggest confounders:
    • Core temperature ≥36 °C
    • SBP ≥100–110 mmHg, MAP ≥75–80 mmHg
  • Exclude drugs/toxins/metabolic: tox screen if indicated, EtOH ≤80 mg/dL, drug levels or ≥5 half-lives, no residual paralysis (TOF), correct severe electrolyte/acid-base issues. Miotic pupils (<2 mm) → think toxin.

3 Clinical Examination (Brainstem Focus)

Test for absence of all brainstem reflexes. Any brain- or brainstem-mediated response rules out BD/DNC. Spinal reflexes are allowed.

  • Unresponsiveness: No response to visual, auditory, or tactile stimuli. Apply pressure to supraorbital notch and to all four extremities (proximal and distal). Watch carefully for any facial movement or limb response.
  • Pupils (CN II/III): Midsize to dilated with no light response. Miotic pupils (<2 mm) are a red flag for possible drug intoxication.
  • Corneal (V1 afferent → CN VII efferent): Saline flush at the external border of the iris. If no response, escalate to gentle pressure with a cotton swab on a 4×4. Look for any eyelid movement.
  • Oculocephalic (doll’s eyes; CN VIII → III/VI): Confirm no C-spine precautions, secure the ETT, rotate the head briskly side to side. Eyes must stay mid-position (no movement).
  • Oculovestibular / cold calorics (if OCR cannot be done or is absent): Otoscopy first to confirm intact tympanic membrane. HOB 30°. 50 mL ice-cold water through a 20–22 Fr catheter over ~60 seconds. Wait 5 minutes and observe for any nystagmus or eye movement before doing the other side. No nystagmus = consistent with BD/DNC.
  • Gag (CN IX/X): Stimulate bilateral posterior pharyngeal wall with a rigid suction catheter or tongue depressor.
  • Cough (CN IX/X): Stimulate the carina with a suction catheter through the ETT.

Spinal reflexes that do NOT rule out BD/DNC: triple flexion, undulating toes, abdominal reflex, cremasteric reflex, Lazarus sign.

4 Apnea Testing

At least one apnea test is required. Two examinations reduce the chance of error (no mandated interval in adults, but 24 hours is common practice).

  • Before starting: SBP >110, MAP >80, pH 7.35–7.45, PaCO₂ 35–45 (or chronic baseline), preoxygenate 100% O₂ ≥10 min aiming for PaO₂ >200.
  • How to test: Disconnect from the ventilator and give 100% O₂ via (1) catheter at 5–6 L/min placed in the ETT above the carina, (2) CPAP on the ventilator with alarms off, or (3) bag-valve mask with PEEP valve.
  • At exactly 8 minutes draw an ABG, then subsequent ABGs until pH ≤7.30 and PaCO₂ ≥60 (or ≥20 above the patient’s known baseline).
  • Time of death = the time the ABG crosses both thresholds.
  • Abort if: SBP <100 (or you cannot support it), MAP <75, SpO₂ <85, or arrhythmia with hemodynamic instability.

AAN Statement 24b / 25f: In chronic hypercapnia, the pre-test PaCO₂ must be at the patient’s known chronic baseline (if unknown, use estimated baseline + ancillary testing).

5 Ancillary Testing

Not routinely needed. Use only when the clinical exam or apnea test cannot be completed or interpreted (AAN Statement 27). Preferred studies: 4-vessel catheter angiography or radionuclide perfusion scintigraphy. Do not use EEG, CTA, or MRA as ancillary tests for BD/DNC.

Key AAN points from your notes
  • One examination is sufficient; two examinations reduce error.
  • Statement 24b / 25f: chronic hypercapnia baseline must be used for apnea testing.
  • Statement 27: indications for ancillary testing.
